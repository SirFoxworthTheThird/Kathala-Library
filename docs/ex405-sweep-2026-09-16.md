# EX-405 sweep of the shipped library

*16 September 2026. All 41 books. Method, findings, and the changes made in
response — see **What was changed** at the end.*

The reader run that produced the Monte Cristo fix read one book. This checks the
other forty against the same rule:

> **EX-405 — Preserve reading-mode spoilers.** Data visible before an event must
> not reveal later deaths, identities, alliances, locations, ownership, or
> outcomes.

## Method

A script mirrors `src/db/hooks/useReading.ts` — the same appearance sources, the
same `chapter.number + sortOrder / 1e6` key, the same map-gateway propagation —
so "revealed at chapter N" means here what it means in the app. For every record
a reader can open, it compares *when the record becomes readable* against *what
the record says*.

Three passes, kept apart because they fail differently:

| pass | asks | raw | after filtering |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | does a record name a **character** the gate reveals later? | 1,753 | 163 |
| B | does a record readable early use **outcome language** — "later", "eventually", "is revealed to be"? | — | 168 |
| C | does a record name a character the gate reveals **never**? | — | 212 |

The filtering matters more than the raw count. Two corrections took pass A from
noise to signal:

- **A place named early is not a spoiler.** The gate reveals a marker when a
  scene is *set* there, and deliberately does not reveal a place merely
  mentioned. Gondor is named in chapter 1 of *The Two Towers* and first hosts a
  scene at 15. Dropping places as spoilers removed about two thirds of pass A.
- **A chapter is not a fixed distance.** One chapter ahead is a long way in
  *Alice* and nothing in the 153-chapter *Wise Man's Fear*. The threshold is
  now both: more than a chapter, and more than a tenth of the book.

Pass B is only run against **standing** records — a character's description, a
relationship's label, a goal, a thread, a lore page — the things readable from
the moment their subject appears and unchanged thereafter. It is not run against
**pinned** records, a snapshot's notes or a knowledge fact, because "reveals
himself" in a note attached to the scene where he reveals himself is not a
spoiler, it is the scene.

Calibration: *The Count of Monte Cristo*, freshly fixed, scores A:1 B:3 C:0.
That is the noise floor, and everything below is read against it.

## What the sweep did not find

Worth stating, because it was the first guess:

- **Lore pages are gated correctly.** All 41 books set `visibleFromEventId` on
  every page; none is fully ungated.
- *The Two Towers* carries 48 lore pages readable from chapter 1, which look
  alarming until you read them: they are chapter summaries of *The Fellowship of
  the Ring*. A reader of volume two has read volume one. Judged acceptable.
- Pass C's 212 hits are 188 from two books (*The Two Towers*, *The Wise Man's
  Fear*), where a character record exists for someone who never appears in a
  scene. `isRevealed` fails closed, so reading mode never lists them. That is a
  completeness gap, not a spoiler — EX-403/404 territory.

## What it did find

**Plot threads are the systemic violation, and neither pass A nor B was what
caught them.** They surfaced while checking pass B's hits against the records.

A thread is revealed at the first event carrying it, but its **name and
description describe the whole arc**. So a thread that appears once in chapter 2
and runs to chapter 15 publishes chapter 15 to a reader at chapter 2. The app's
own source already says this — `useReading.ts`: *"Their names are among the
sharpest a world holds — 'The Philosopher's Stone Mystery' gives away the book on
its own."* The gate hides the thread until it appears; nothing constrains what it
then says.

290 threads and motifs across the 41 books become readable in the first quarter
of their book and run into the last quarter. That is a count of *candidates*,
not of violations, and it does not move when a description is reworded — it is a
property of which scenes carry the thread. Most are thematic and harmless —
*"The Wrath of Achilles"* announces itself in the poem's first line. These are
the ones that named a **concealed** fact:

### Outcome language in standing descriptions

Separately, 168 standing records use forward-looking language, **76 of them
readable in the first fifth of their book**. The pattern is a description that
tells you what a thing will later do:

- *Peter Pan*, ch. 3 of 17 — Acorn Button: "worn at her neck and **later stopping
  an arrow**"
- *Dracula*, ch. 2 of 27 — Dracula / Jonathan Harker: "…and **later becomes the
  object of his vengeance**"
- *The Moonstone*, ch. 2 of 57 — Franklin Blake: "…**eventually turns the inquiry
  upon his own missing memory**" — the solution, on his character card
- *The Woman in White*, ch. 3 of 62 — Professor Pesca: "whose past association
  **later exposes Fosco**"
- *Pride and Prejudice*, ch. 3 of 61 — Darcy / Bingley: "…and **later helps repair
  the harm**"
- *Robinson Crusoe*, ch. 2 of 20 — Crusoe / Xury: "though Crusoe **later accepts
  Xury's sale** into conditional service"

Some pass-B hits are not about the story at all — *About the Local Maps*,
*Editorial Calendar*, *Intentional Feature Decisions* use "later" about editions
and map generation. Those are authoring notes, not spoilers. They do raise the
separate question of whether authoring notes should be readable in reading mode,
which the first blind reader run also reported.

## Limits of this sweep

It reads records, not books. It cannot tell a concealed fact from one the prose
has already stated on page one — that judgement is in the table above and it is
mine, not the script's. The script's job was to get 1,753 candidates down to a
few hundred worth reading. Every row above was then checked against the record
in the `.pwk`.

It also does not check the direction that matters most and is hardest: a
description that spoils **without** naming anybody or using a forward-looking
word. That is how Monte Cristo's violations read — *"a young sailor who becomes
the Count"* — and only the reader run found those.

## What was changed

**59 plot threads across 14 books**, and **35 standing descriptions across 12
books** — 94 records across 17 of the 41 books. Every replacement says only what is known at the
record's first appearance, and each was written against that first scene, not
from memory of the novel. The patch asserted the old text before replacing it,
so a description that had drifted would have failed rather than been silently
skipped.

Some examples, with the chapter the text becomes readable at:

| book | was | is |
|---|---|---|
| Jekyll and Hyde, ch. 1 | *Jekyll's Double Life* — "…destroys both identities" | *Jekyll's Reticence* — "What Jekyll will not explain about his own household, and what his oldest friend makes of the silence." |
| Hound of the Baskervilles, ch. 2 | *Stapleton's Conspiracy* — "Stapleton hides his identity, marriage, hound…" | *Sir Charles at the Moor-Gate* — "…as though waiting for somebody, and the cigar ash says he waited some time." |
| Hound of the Baskervilles | Stapleton / Sir Charles: "Murderer and victim" | "Neighbours on the moor" |
| The Woman in White, ch. 4 | *The Identity Exchange* | *The Woman on the Road* |
| Philosopher's Stone, ch. 1 | *Voldemort's Return* — "…regain a body and power through the Philosopher's Stone" | *The Night the Potters Died* — "…his own disappearance and a child who should not have survived it." |
| Moby-Dick, ch. 1 | "…through the coffin that saves Ishmael" | "A shared bed at the Spouter-Inn turns dread of a stranger into the closest friendship either man has." |
| Peter Pan, ch. 3 | Acorn Button — "…and later stopping an arrow" | "An acorn Peter gives Wendy in exchange for a kiss, which she wears on a chain at her neck." |

Pass B falls from 168 hits to 135. `npm run gate` is green — 684 tests, nothing
broken and nothing silently fixed — and `library/index.json` was regenerated for
the 17 books whose `dataBytes` moved.

### Checked and left alone

- **Fellowship: Gollum / Déagol, "Murderer and Victim".** Flagged, then
  withdrawn: the relationship becomes visible at `lotr-ev-deagol`, the scene in
  which Gandalf tells the reader that Sméagol murdered Déagol for the Ring. The
  label says exactly what the reader has just been told.
- **Harry Potter: Voldemort / James Potter, "Murderer and victim"** — visible at
  chapter 15, and the reader learns it in chapter 4.
- The thematic threads in *The Iliad*, *The Odyssey*, *Jane Eyre*, *Journey to
  the West* and *The Wonderful Wizard of Oz*, which describe a shape rather than
  an event.
- The authoring notes (*About the Local Maps*, *Editorial Calendar*,
  *Intentional Feature Decisions*). They use "later" about editions and map
  generation, not about the story. Whether they should be readable in reading
  mode at all is a separate question, raised by the first blind reader run.

### Still open

135 pass-B hits remain, in the 24 books not touched here and in records judged
thematic on this pass. They were read and left rather than missed, but the line
between "thematic" and "gives it away" is a judgement made once, quickly, by one
reader. A second pass should start with the books that were never opened:
*Around the World in Eighty Days*, *The Invisible Man*, *The Time Machine*,
*Jane Eyre* and *The Three Musketeers* carry the most.

The structural fault is also still there. Rewording a thread stops it *saying*
the ending; nothing stops the next one from doing it again. The rule that would
hold is mechanical — a thread's description may not name an entity the gate
reveals after the thread itself — and that is a test this repository could run,
not a paragraph in `AUTHORING.md`.
