# Verified narrative notes — authoring work in progress

These notes derive from reading the local Gutenberg text, not a synopsis. They are not published story content or a completed checklist.

## Frame and Chapters I–VIII

- Frame: Christmas fireside gathering; Douglas sends a key to fetch his locked manuscript from London; it arrives the third day and is read the fourth night. The manuscript is a thin faded-red gilt-edged album. Douglas knew the governess through his sister, not at Bly. The governess is twenty, a Hampshire parson’s youngest daughter; her guardian interview is Harley Street, and Bly is explicitly in Essex. His condition is never to trouble him. Frame geography is unspecified and must not become a precise invented address.
- I: June coach arrival and fly to Bly; Mrs. Grose and Flora welcome her. First-night uncertain cry/footsteps do not identify a present child. Flora’s bed is later installed in the governess’s room. Flora guides a house tour through crooked stairs to the tower summit.
- II: Uncle’s enclosure arrives late second evening; she reads the headmaster’s dismissal alone before bed. Mrs. Grose cannot read. Flora interrupts their schoolroom discussion while practising round O letters. Subsequent stair discussion mentions an unnamed predecessor, holiday departure and death, with cause unknown.
- III: Miles arrives at the coach inn; Flora travels with the governess to fetch him. She and Grose privately agree not to answer uncle or school. Weeks of happy lessons precede an evening lawn walk and first unnamed man seen atop the old tower. Observer and perceived figure occupy different map levels.
- IV: She conceals the sighting from Grose on returning to the hall. Sunday rain delays church; she retrieves gloves repaired over tea from a dining chair and sees the same man outside the window. She searches outside and stands in his position; Grose sees her through the pane and is startled.
- V: On the terrace, the governess describes a handsome hatless red-haired man with pale long face, red whiskers, dark arched brows and borrowed master’s clothes. Grose identifies Peter Quint, formerly valet and in charge of Bly, now dead. Neither apparition is present during this identification conversation.
- VI: Closed schoolroom conversation continues until midnight; Grose has not personally seen an apparition. She recalls Quint’s intimacy with Miles and reports his inquest death on an icy village road after drinking. A week later governess and Flora play at the lake; Miles is indoors reading, not present at the lake. Flora makes a flat-wood boat and wooden mast, no sail. Governess senses someone across the lake before looking.
- VII: Two hours later governess reports the black-clad beautiful woman and claims Flora saw her. Grose identifies Miss Jessel and recounts her improper relationship with Quint; the precise cause of Jessel’s death remains unknown. Do not encode the governess’s claim about Flora as confirmed knowledge.
- VIII: Governess remembers Flora’s affectionate response, then questions Grose at night about Miles spending months with Quint. Grose describes denials and impudence but uncertainty about what the children knew. They agree to wait rather than accuse.

## Chapters IX–XIV

- IX: A lull of lessons, recitations, private theatricals and schoolroom piano. Governess reads Fielding’s *Amelia* beside Flora’s curtained bed late at night. She locks the bedroom and moves into the lobby; her candle goes out, dawn light reveals Quint at the half-landing. They stare silently; she feels resolve rather than fear, and he turns down the stair and disappears. Do not give the children ghost knowledge from her interpretation.
- X: Returning, she finds Flora behind the bedroom blind, not in bed. Flora says she looked for her and saw nobody; the governess suspects a lie but does not accuse. Later nocturnal patrol: she sees Jessel seated on a lower stair, bowed with head in hands, then vanish. On the eleventh night after Quint, about one o’clock, Flora is again at the window and the candle extinguished. The governess leaves quietly, hesitates at Miles’s door, then enters the unused square old-tower bedroom, unbolts a shutter and sees Miles on the lawn. Her initial expectation of Jessel outside is not an actual sighting. A supposed person above her is inference, not confirmed Quint snapshot.
- XI: Late next afternoon, governess and Grose converse on terrace while Miles reads aloud with his arm round Flora on the lawn within sight. The chapter recounts the preceding night: governess retrieves Miles via terrace, takes him upstairs to his unslept-in room. Miles says he read until midnight and went outside to be thought bad; he arranged Flora’s window watching to attract the governess. This is an explicit admitted plan, not proof of supernatural conspiracy. Distinguish earlier remembered event calendar pins from later terrace account.
- XII: Same terrace discussion; children visibly stroll on the lawn and smile at the women. Governess asserts four-way meetings and a demonic design as her interpretation. Grose urges contacting the uncle; governess fears ridicule and threatens to leave if Grose appeals on her behalf. Uncle is mentioned, not present. Children are spatially distant and have their own reading/strolling statuses, not the adults’ alarm.
- XIII: Another month of avoided subjects; summer passes into autumn. Children ask about her Hampshire home and uncle’s visit; their letters are retained by the governess, not posted. She privately rehearses naming the dead in her room and recoils. There are no new visible apparitions: perceived visitors during silences are unverified inference, no ghost snapshots. Calendar is approximate except explicit intervals.
- XIV: Frosty autumn Sunday church walk, Miles with governess and Flora with Grose ahead. Miles asks when he returns to school and demands boys’ company and more life. At the churchyard tomb, the two are alone after Flora and Grose enter church. He challenges the uncle’s indifference, says he will make him come, and enters church alone. Add a distinct churchyard marker; do not keep Grose/Flora in the private confrontation.

## Chapters XV–XXIV

- XV: Alone in churchyard, she considers escape, walks home through park and collapses on the lowest stair. In the upstairs schoolroom, she sees Jessel at her table, head supported by hands; the figure rises and disappears after the governess addresses her. Jessel does not audibly speak. This encounter decides her to stay.
- XVI: Before tea, housekeeper’s room, fire and smell of baked bread. Grose says children requested silence about her absence. Governess describes an imagined conversation with Jessel as a talk about damnation; distinguish this from XV’s silent encounter. They decide she writes to the uncle tonight. Grose proposes the bailiff because she cannot write, then asks governess to do it.
- XVII: Stormy evening, governess hesitates over blank paper beside peaceful Flora, then visits awake Miles. He asks for new school and privacy; she presses for confession and saving him. Gust/chill, shriek and darkness; Miles explicitly says he blew out the candle. No ghost actually seen in this bedroom scene.
- XVIII: Next morning lessons excellent; letter sealed in her pocket. After early dinner Miles plays schoolroom piano; she forgets to watch Flora. Flora missing from rooms and Grose’s care. Ten-minute searches end hall; letter placed on hall table for Luke. Governess and Grose leave bareheaded. Claims Miles is with Quint and Flora with Jessel are inferences, not visible sightings.
- XIX: Lake embarkation half a mile from house, long narrow oblong pond. Empty mooring; women walk round overgrown shore about ten minutes, find flat-bottomed boat with two short thick oars concealed and tied to a fence stake near gate. Flora nearby plucks withered fern. Grose embraces her; governess directly asks where Jessel is. Add mooring and far-bank fence gate markers.
- XX: On far side with Flora and Grose, governess sees Jessel on opposite bank, the very bank women had left: use near-bank position, not always far-bank. Grose explicitly sees nobody; Flora denies ever seeing and calls governess cruel, asks removal. Grose takes her home; governess collapses alone by water, returns dusk, boat gone. Flora’s belongings removed from governess room; Flora stays with Grose. Eight o’clock Miles joins governess schoolroom hearth, two silent hours. Do not snapshot absent Flora/Grose there.
- XXI: Predawn bedroom Grose reports feverish Flora, frightened of present governess. They arrange Flora’s departure with Grose to uncle that morning. Grose reports shocking language and professes belief; this is not visual corroboration. Governess’s letter vanished; Grose recounts Luke saying he never touched/noticed it. Theft and school theft are suspicions at this point. Luke mentioned, not physically present. Governess letter contained bare demand for interview, not full ghost account.
- XXII: Governess comes down after carriage has departed; no direct on-page departure sighting required. She tours house/grounds apprehensively, Miles absent, no lessons. Miles’s breakfast with sister and Grose is reported history, not a present event. Alone together at downstairs dining room meal of roast mutton, Miles asks Flora’s illness/travel; attendants leave; he says they are alone.
- XXIII: Explicit November scenery at dining window. Miles says he enjoyed freedom; governess says stayed to hear him confess. He promises later, wants to go see Luke, twirling his brought-in hat (a verified item, unlike an invented abandoned night-walk hat). She asks whether he took yesterday’s letter.
- XXIV: Quint at outside dining window as governess draws Miles away. Miles admits taking, opening and burning letter, found nothing about him. Quint vanishes temporarily. Miles denies stealing at school, says he said things to boys he liked and they were repeated to masters; precise words withheld. Governess briefly fears his innocence. Quint appears again; Miles asks if Jessel is there, then names Peter Quint and asks where, seeing only quiet day. She catches/holds him; after a minute his heart stopped. Only final event marks Miles dead. Death cause and supernatural explanation must remain unresolved, not an invented possession diagnosis.
