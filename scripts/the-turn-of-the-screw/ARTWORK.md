# Artwork record

All 61 selected PNGs under `library/the-turn-of-the-screw/art/` are original
illustrations generated for this example with OpenAI's built-in image-generation
tool on 15 September 2026. They are repository assets; the PWK uses no remote
entity-image links and requires no PWB bundle.

The common direction was a mature 1850s English literary illustration: restrained
watercolour over fine etched or graphite linework, muted earth, moss, candle, and
storm colours, period clothing and objects, natural proportions, no photography,
no modern objects, no text, and no childish/cartoon treatment. Every portrait,
object, and place was prompted separately with its source-derived subject details,
then visually inspected. Portraits were required to have distinct faces and poses;
objects were required to depict the named object rather than a map or generic prop.

The five maps were prompted independently as functional, period-inspired maps:
southern England; an original interpreted Bly estate plan; two original overhead
manor floorplans; and an original roof plan. The estate and floorplans were not
derived from a real house and are explicitly identified as interpretation in Lore.
The estate-map revision removed invented decorative prose. Selected corrected
variants also replace an inaccurate toy boat with a mast but no sail, replace a
loose-page manuscript with the source's thin red gilt-edged album, and replace an
incorrect two-bed tower room with the source's unused square bedroom.

Prompts were composed by the generation calls from each entity's name and
non-spoiling ledger description in `world-ledger.mjs`, plus the common direction
above. The specific visual requirements for Peter Quint (hatless, red close curls,
pale long face, red whiskers, borrowed smart clothes), Miss Jessel (pale and
haggard in poor black mourning), Flora's bare wooden toy, the flat-bottomed boat
with two short oars, the shared governess/Flora bedroom, and the old tower bedroom
were included verbatim in their individual prompts.
