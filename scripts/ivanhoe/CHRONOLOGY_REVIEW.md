# Ivanhoe chronology review — reviewed editorial reconstruction

The relative-day sequence, source anchors, parallel strands, retrospective events,
523 dramatic-pressure ratings and actual calendar presentation have been reviewed.
June1194 and interpolated clock times are editorial choices, not a verified
historical itinerary. The129 temporal witnesses distinguish prediction, threat,
song, simile and recalled acts from elapsed time. Source paragraphs are zero-based.
Evidence: temporal-classifications.mjs, tension-review.mjs,
qa/chronology-source-evidence.json, qa/calendar-clock-manual.json and the source
regressions. Packaged-file and repository gates are recorded separately.

## Reviewed relative sequence

- I.8: setting sun at opening glade. III.3–12: delayed evening meal.
  III.21: tournament begins on the second day from that evening. VI.3 refers
  to a cup after midnight; VI.28 explicitly sleeps until earliest sunbeams.
  VI.59 calls the new morning summer. Do not force a historically authenticated
  Gregorian day/month on the romance or treat the epigraph as a timed event.
- VII.6: appointed tournament morning. VIII.21: first/second/third-day programme;
  IX.54 and X.40–XI.63: dusk/evening after first contests. XII.3: next morning.
  XIII.30 explicitly brings the planned third-day archery forward to the second
  day, cancelling tomorrow's entertainments. XIV: evening banquet. XV.20:
  prospective ambush tomorrow is a plan, not a completed attack tonight.
- XVI.3: the Black Knight spends the post-melee night at an unnamed hostelry;
  XVI.4: departs next morning, loses time in the woods, reaches hermitage near
  evening. XVII's feast lasts toward midnight, corroborated by XX.19/67.
- XVIII.3 rewinds to the unhelmeting. XVIII.17: Cedric's party leaves the
  abbey after feasting late and breakfast next morning. XVIII.28 calls Wilfred's
  fall yesterday; XVIII.39 is noon rest. XIX.3: late forest journey. XXI.3:
  captors lose their way, march through darkness and reach the right way only
  after summer dawn. XXI.26: castle seen in morning sun. Therefore the castle
  arrival must not be dated to the same afternoon as the ambush.
- XXI.43 / XXIII.4 establish around noon at the castle; XXII.7 says Isaac has
  waited nearly three hours. XXV.42's before-noon execution is a captor's threat,
  not a witnessed execution or reliable completed deadline. XXVI.19 recalls
  the recent noon meal; XXVII.45's24hour delay is the Baron’s proposed stratagem.
- XXVIII.3 explicitly retrogrades to the Ashby rescue; paragraph12 reaches
  farther back to the road to Ashby. Paragraphs19–55 cover post-melee evening,
  recovery, sedative and next morning; paragraphs56–59 retrace journey/capture
  and castle arrival. Paragraph60 reconnects with the siege alarm. These13
  retrospective scenes are now flagged; paragraph60 is the resumed frame.
  XXVIII.31/50: eight days is a recovery forecast/instruction, not elapsed days.
- XXXI.92: castle burns against evening sky. XXXII.7/46: following morning,
  yesterday's dead. XXXIV.4 explicitly is the morning after the castle falls.
- XXXV.3: Isaac's attempted journey ends with Nathan lodging him, four miles
  from Templestowe. XXXV.4: next morning departure. XXXVI.53: noon trial.
  XXXVIII.18/28/48: combat is assigned the third day from the trial, not held
  immediately. XXXIX.3: evening prayer after the trial.
- XL.3 resumes the Black Knight after the forest distribution, goes to Botolph
  and departs on the succeeding morning. XLI.11 gives an intended further24h
  muster interval; XLII.42/58 calls Athelstane's deprivation three days, without
  an exact start/end clock. Count may be inclusive; do not silently force it.
- XLIV.50: consent occurs before a seven-day court stay has elapsed; the source
  does not state an exact wedding date. XLIV.54 fixes the farewell visit to the
  second morning after the wedding. Retain that relation in any editorial clock.
