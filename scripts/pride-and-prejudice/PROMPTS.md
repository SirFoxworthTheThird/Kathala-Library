# Pride and Prejudice artwork direction

Each asset was generated independently with an asset-specific prompt. All prompts shared this baseline: original naturalistic oil-painted realism; refined Regency portraiture or landscape painting; restrained early nineteenth-century book-illustration texture; England circa 1811–1812; expressive but unsentimental characterization; no readable text, logos, borders, or watermarks.

## Cover

- `cover`: Elizabeth and Darcy walking above Pemberley, full-length figures, clear emotional reserve and recognition, portrait-oriented composition with title space but no rendered typography.

## Characters

Prompts used each PWK character’s recorded age, role, relationships, and personality. The Bennet sisters were differentiated as Jane (22, serene), Elizabeth (20, intelligent and lively), Mary (18, serious and bookish), Kitty (17, uncertain), and Lydia (15, exuberant). Darcy, Bingley, Wickham, Collins, Colonel Fitzwilliam, Colonel Forster, and Denny received distinct civilian, clerical, regular-army, or militia cues. Married women, servants, companions, tradespeople, and landed families received age- and class-appropriate dress and settings. Filename keys correspond exactly to character IDs. Corrective prompts explicitly prohibited post-Regency sleeve silhouettes for Mrs Bennet and Mrs Philips.

## Items

Each item prompt named its exact narrative object and physical context: folded or sealed letters, ball invitation, dance card, Fordyce volume, Gardiner carriage, Pemberley portrait, settlement papers, and wedding ring. Paper marks were required to remain illegible. `pemberley-portrait` used the approved `characters/darcy` master as its sole identity reference.

## Locations

Each location prompt named its exact region, season, social function, architecture, and narrative mood. Hertfordshire locations share softened lowland light and brick or modest Georgian construction; Kent contrasts Hunsford’s modest parsonage with formal Rosings; Derbyshire uses limestone, wooded heights, river valleys, and Pemberley’s natural harmony; London is denser and urban; Brighton combines the early-Regency town, Channel coast, bathing culture, and temporary militia life without later piers, railways, or Victorian resort architecture.

The 24 location keys are: `hertfordshire-portal`, `london-portal`, `kent-portal`, `derbyshire-portal`, `brighton-portal`, `newcastle`, `longbourn`, `meryton`, `assembly`, `netherfield`, `lucas-lodge`, `philips-house`, `oakham-mount`, `gardiner-house`, `darcy-london`, `wickham-lodgings`, `hunsford`, `rosings`, `rosings-grove`, `pemberley`, `lambton`, `lambton-inn`, `brighton-camp`, and `brighton-town`.
