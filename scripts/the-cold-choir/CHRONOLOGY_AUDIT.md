# Chronology decisions

The manuscript is unchanged. Its three historical strands retain their own
numeric clocks. The following are editorial day placements, not a claim that
the source specifies every intervening date. Immediate cuts share a day;
`sortOrder` preserves their order. `travelDays` is the nonnegative difference
between successive scene positions on the same strand, not necessarily travel.

| 32 | 40.75 | Explicit twenty days after the duct meeting. |

| 38 | 60, 60.25 | Same final maneuver; descent placed later that day. |
| 39 | 60.75 | New-year anchor: forty days of Ledger custody after 20.75. |

The source's local cues conflict: twenty days from the duct meeting to the
navigator meeting, plus the eleven-day recollection in Burn, total 31 days;
the earlier forecast and final custody statement both say forty. This edition
prioritizes the repeated forty-day custody interval and the twenty-day meeting
cue. It does not silently rewrite the eleven-day sentence. Its unsatisfied
interval is explicitly an unresolved manuscript discrepancy. The precise
quarter-day placement of the final maneuver is editorial.

Mercy's offset is -60.75: its first Landfall Day is day 0 of the common clock.
The 365-day Shipyear is an editorial display convention, not a source calendar.

The Quiet offset is 742×365. Its calendar placement within that year is
editorial. The interlude says “seven hundred and forty-two years later” next
to the 311 AL frame, while surrounding headings identify the expedition as
742 AL. The heading controls the timeline; the inconsistent connective is
preserved verbatim and is not used to invent a fourth era.

The reader-facing chronology explanation is revealed only at the final scene
because discussing the Ledger, surviving sleepers, and awakening reveals outcomes.
