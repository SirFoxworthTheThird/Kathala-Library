# Map source and visual review

The five geographic charts use [Natural Earth 1:10m land polygons](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/10m-physical-vectors/10m-land/) and [1:10m shaded relief](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/10m-shaded-relief/10m-shaded-relief-basic/). [Natural Earth releases both as public-domain map data](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/). The checked-in `map-source/ne_10m_land.geojson` is 10,157,965 bytes, SHA-256 `1ac90796408bc6ad6911d69448485d3c4dbf2190370080368a09976e1c9f7416`; `map-source/SR_LR.zip` is 25,179,752 bytes, SHA-256 `e3aa47b13aff26e1b4b3792a94100d8667d3147046aeac7840be00a15f839d18`. The raster is Natural Earth Shaded Relief Basic, medium size, version 3.2.0, with a 16,200 × 8,100 global TIFF. The GeoJSON was downloaded from the Natural Earth vector project's GitHub repository on 11 October 2026. The original 1:110m GeoJSON remains as an audit reference; the current renderer uses 1:10m.

`render-maps.py` crops actual relief within each equirectangular WGS84 chart extent, masks it to the vector coast, draws latitude and longitude lines, and overlays the editorial voyage track and spoiler-safe location pins. The 1497–1499 sea track is approximate, based on the voyage journal cited in `CHRONOLOGY.md`; it is not a surviving plotted ship log. The Malabar route ends at sea off Calicut instead of crossing the land. Names not yet disclosed by the poem remain in Kathala's gated markers rather than being burned into the image. The ocean chart spans longitude −28° to 88°, latitude −43° to 48°; Portugal, East Africa, Malabar and England have more detailed regional extents. England belongs to Veloso's embedded tale and is not part of Gama's itinerary.

Calicut's local sites have no surviving surveyed placement in the poem. Its relief, coastline and paths are therefore an **interpretive relative plan**, not historical topography. The Island of Love has fictional contour lines and a shore drawn for the allegory, with no Earth coordinates or claimed elevations. The divine setting is likewise symbolic: a mountain-like domain for Olympus and a sea area for marine action; its marker positions do not locate the gods on Earth. These three layers say so in their captions. Their generated contour intervals are visual rather than measurements. The map images serve navigation through the poem and must not be used as historical geographic evidence.

`map-marker-coordinates.json` records pixel positions with **y measured from the bottom edge**, as Kathala requires. The renderer computes coordinates from WGS84 for geographic maps and documented relative positions for the three interpretive layers. The eight rendered PNGs are the only downloadable map assets in `library/os-lusiadas/maps/`; the two Natural Earth data files and renderer reproduce them. `update-map-manifest.mjs` records each final PNG hash and review note in `artwork-manifest.json`.

## Topographic revision, 11 October 2026

The prior illustrated-plate revision obscured geography and made invented layouts look arbitrary. Its eight generated background plates were rejected and removed from the release. The current charts use actual coastlines and shaded terrain, with a restrained atlas palette, graticules, visible route, and readable pins. All eight PNGs were opened at full map resolution after regeneration. The ocean map visibly rounds the Cape and reaches Malabar; the Portugal, East Africa, Malabar and England regions show recognizable landforms. The three interpretive layers have differentiated water, coast, contour relief, and sites; the divine layer no longer draws false overland roads between sea and sky domains. The Island has a curved coast rather than a coarse polygon.

The revised PWK was imported into the local Kathala development UI on 11 October. Its initial reading view hid 78 characters and 68 places not yet encountered. A separate QA copy exposed the full atlas for visual inspection: all eight layers rendered with their site pins at normal viewport size, including the ocean track, regional relief, and the three explicitly interpretive terrains. An isolated-ID import confirmed the ocean layer had **zero** application straight-line routes and the painted maritime track stayed at sea. The Lisbon marker's **Open Sub-map** control entered Portugal successfully. Browser console errors: zero. The older import remains in the browser and retains its own obsolete route records; its state is not evidence about the revised PWK. `validate-maps.mjs` and `validate-book.mjs` also check pixel bounds and bottom-edge placement.
