# Chronology audit — Os Lusíadas

The canto and stanza order is the reading order. It is not a continuous physical clock. `chronology.mjs` assigns every candidate scene a clock and a numeric calendar position without changing its printed position. A scene in Gama's account of earlier sailing shares the voyage clock with the framing Melinde audience, but is explicitly marked retrospective. Recounted Portuguese history, Veloso's tale, divine and allegorical action, and the poet's addresses each have a separate clock. A change of coast or speaker within the ongoing voyage does not create another timeline.

## Calendar convention

Kathala receives a twelve-month, 365-day editorial grid beginning in 1000. Leap days are omitted so the numeric day positions are reproducible. The displayed year is meaningful; a displayed day is authoritative only when an external anchor below gives it. Every other day is a navigational interpolation or the first day of an undated year. Scenes within a single encounter may occupy fractional positions on the same display day. `travelDays` is the nonnegative distance from the preceding scene **on that clock**, with backward reported jumps represented by an explicit time pin and zero elapsed days. The printed scenes remain in canto order.

The poet's direct address is placed in 1572, the publication year of the source edition. Its 1 January display day is a year-only placeholder. Gama's historical survey uses the approximate year of each episode, often 1 January as another year-only placeholder. The Twelve of England is a romance told aboard ship; 1400 is a deliberately approximate story-era placeholder, not an assertion of a dated tournament. The Island of Love is allegorical and has no known earthly position or date; 1 June 1499 locates its sequence in an approximate homeward interval in the UI. The poem does not supply the contemporary journal's detailed homeward ports, so that interval is not presented as a mapped itinerary.

## Voyage anchors

E. G. Ravenstein's edition of the contemporaneous first-voyage journal, [*A Journal of the First Voyage of Vasco da Gama, 1497–1499*](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/46440/old/46440-h/46440-h.htm), documents 8 July 1497 departure; 4–7 November at St Helena Bay; 22 November at the Cape; 2 March 1498 at Mozambique; 7 April at Mombasa; 14 April at Melinde; 24 April departure for the ocean crossing; 18 May first Indian landfall; 20 May off Calicut; and 29–30 August departure from Calicut. These are date anchors for analogous episodes in Camões, not a claim that poem and journal are identical accounts. An editor using the manuscript alone can reproduce every intermediate position by the anchor interpolation in `chronology.mjs`.

The journal records Nicolau Coelho's vessel back at Lisbon on 10 July 1499, but Ravenstein notes conflicting evidence for **Vasco da Gama's own return** in late August or September. The canto X return is displayed at 29 August 1499 as a rough position only. The poem moves directly from the Island of Love to the Tagus; this edition does not add unwritten homeward episodes.

## Historical anchors

The broad early monarchy positions are editorial approximate years. The battle of Ourique is positioned at 25 July 1139, the traditional date; its particulars and the visionary account belong to Camões's narrative, not a modern event reconstruction. The Inês de Castro episode is positioned in 1355; [Portugal's Património Cultural](https://www.patrimoniocultural.gov.pt/mosteiro-de-santa-clara-a-velha/) identifies her execution in that year at Santa Clara-a-Velha. Aljubarrota is positioned at 14 August 1385; [Museu da Comunidade Concelhia da Batalha](https://www.museubatalha.com/pt/noticias-noticias-mccb/346/-comemoracoes-do-dia-internacional-dos-museus-) gives that date. Lisbon's capture, Ceuta, the reign transitions, and the succession crisis use approximate years to keep the historical sequence legible. Canto III places Egas Moniz's episode in 1128 on the display grid alongside Afonso's claim to power. This keeps the recounted sequence nondecreasing while acknowledging that the poem compresses events whose historical chronology is disputed. The validator checks the historical clock for reversals.

The chronology is an editorial reading aid. It must be restated concisely in the downloaded world's Lore so a reader does not mistake these display dates for exact days supplied by the poem.
