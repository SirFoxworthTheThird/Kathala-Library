# Narrative frames and calendar policy

The poem's printed order is the reading order. It is not a single physical clock. These distinctions govern the production world:

1. **Authorial voice.** The proposition, invocations, dedication, and closing reflections are the poet's addresses. They belong to the composition frame, conventionally pinned to 1572 for calendar display. They are not scenes in which Vasco da Gama or a god is physically standing beside the poet.
2. **Voyage.** The fleet is first seen already off East Africa in Canto I. The departure from Lisbon and the earlier African coast are told later by Gama to Melinde's ruler in Cantos IV–V. Those later printed scenes are retrospective voyage events and must be pinned to earlier voyage time. The Melinde audience itself is the frame in which Gama speaks. Arrival at Calicut and the homeward course continue the voyage after the account.
3. **Reported Portuguese history.** The reigns and battles in Cantos III–IV are Gama's recounting, not people assembled in Melinde. Their historical dates are approximate editorial placements, and their character snapshots must describe only people directly participating in the historical action chosen for a scene. Gama and the Melinde ruler belong to the speaking frame, never to Ourique, Aljubarrota, or Inês's immediate situation. A historical person merely named in a list receives no snapshot at that reading position.
4. **Divine and mythic action.** Olympus, Neptune's council, and Venus's interventions occur in the poem's mythic plane alongside the voyage. They need their own timeline and places. A mortal character should have a snapshot in such a scene only when the poem depicts direct contact there; a god discussing Gama while he is aboard a distant ship is not a shared physical scene.
5. **Reported tales and forecasts.** Veloso's Twelve of England is a tale told aboard a ship; its historical actors are not present on the ship. The island's prophetic song and Tethys's globe disclose future names and deeds when sung or shown, not at the future date of the deeds. Those passages stay anchored to the island encounter for reading-mode reveals. Future figures named in a forecast must not acquire presence snapshots there.

The production calendar uses a simple editorial Gregorian-style year grid. Where the poem gives no day, events have an **approximate date** within the appropriate frame; consecutive stanzas in one encounter may share a date and use fractional days for order. Historical years and the broad 1497–1499 voyage are not a claim that Camões supplied a full diary. The calendar Lore page states the dating assumptions and distinguishes poem time from historical inference. `travelDays` represents elapsed time only within a timeline and is never negative merely because the poem returns to an earlier episode.

The scene-cut ledger uses `poet`, `voyage`, `recounted-voyage`, `recounted-history`, `mythic`, and mixed frame labels as an audit aid. The production generator assigns each scene to a corresponding event clock while the ten canto chapters retain printed reading order. Mixed labels mark a change in the discourse at a stanza boundary; the scene-state ledger records only directly present figures, so a forecast subject or a person named in speech does not acquire a presence snapshot. The separate import draft retains only the canto-order timeline as evidence of the source-first milestone.
